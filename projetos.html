Aircraft & Projects

Explore the aircraft, prototypes, student design studies, and collaborative engineering projects preserved in CEA’s archive. Each project unites mission requirements with physical fabrication and flight testing.

From Cláudio Barros’s foundational gliders to world record-breaking racing monoplanes, CEA develops aerospace engineering capability through hands-on design, hardware fabrication, and experimental flight validation.

CEA-101 · CB-1 • 1964

CB-1 Gaivota

Single-Seat Basic Training Glider — PP-ZTZ

First aircraft engineered at CEA by Prof. Cláudio Barros and completed in 1964. Built from Brazilian freijó wood and aeronautical plywood covered in cotton fabric, it served as the foundational cornerstone of UFMG's practical aeronautical workshop. Fully restored to flight between 2001 and 2003.

CEA-102 · CB-2 • 1975

CB-2 Minuano

High-Performance Standard Class Sailplane — PP-ZPZ

Designed in 1969 and built between 1972 and 1974, Minuano made its maiden flight in December 1975 at Lagoa Santa. A pioneer in structural engineering, it incorporated bonded metal-to-metal and wood-to-metal joints with cellulose acetate honeycomb sandwich skins, achieving an exceptional 38:1 glide ratio.

Conceived by student Rogério Pinto Ribeiro in 1979–1980 within the Aircraft Design academic curriculum. Designed as a lightweight single-seat self-launching glider featuring an auxiliary powerplant, enabling autonomous soaring operations without a towplane.

CETEC-303 · CB-7 • 1988

CB-7 Vesper

Two-Seat Tandem Composite Motor-Glider — PT-ZCB

Collaborative programme between CEA and CETEC/SP initiated in 1985 and flown in 1988. Featuring an innovative twin-boom pusher configuration manufactured in composite materials, it received the prestigious international design award at the 1990 OSTIV Congress in France.

CEA-205 · CB-9 • 1993

CB-9 Curumim

Two-Seat Touring Monoplane & Flying Laboratory

Developed between 1989 and 1992, winning the 1992 Brazilian Ultralight Association Design Prize before making its maiden flight in 1993. Built from freijó wood and pine plywood, Curumim served as an ab initio flight trainer and an instrumented flying laboratory for student research, telemetry calibration, and flight parameter identification.

CEA-308 • 2002 · 4× FAI World Records

CEA-308

Single-Seat World Record High-Efficiency Monoplane

Conceived in early 1999 and built between January 2000 and 2001, CEA-308 completed its maiden flight on 14 May 2002 at 15:50 in Confins, piloted by Daniel Aninger de Barros. Powered by an 80-hp Jabiru 2200 engine, it established four official FAI world records — three speed records and one time-to-climb record — in Class C-1.a/0 under pilot Gunar Armin Halboth.

CEA-311 • 2015 · 5× FAI World Records

CEA-311 Anequim

All-Carbon Racing Monoplane · 521 km/h Record

Pure carbon-fiber racing monoplane engineered at CEA. In August 2015, piloted by Gunar Armin Halboth at the Santa Cruz Air Force Base in Rio de Janeiro, Anequim established five official FAI world speed and climb records in Class C-1.b (sub-500 kg), reaching 521 km/h to become the world's fastest four-cylinder piston aircraft.

Methodology

Design, Build, Test, Fly, Educate

At introductory levels, students formulate mission specifications, review historical designs, and support sizing choices with mathematical physics. As projects mature, student teams engage in detailed CAD drafting, structural fabrication, sensor calibration, and flight-test evaluation. Conceptual coursework and flight-proven aircraft form two complementary pillars of learning.

// HANDS-ON ERGONOMICS

Cockpit Ergonomics & Systems Fitting

Undergraduate aerospace engineering student testing flight-control stick travel, rudder linkage clearance, and cockpit seating geometry inside the welded tubular airframe at the CEA workshop.

// AIRFRAME REDESIGN

Airframe Redesign & Structural Assembly

Structural assembly and aeronautical redesign of a light biplane tubular airframe. Students inspect welded truss joints, wing mounting brackets, and empennage alignment in the CEA workshop.
