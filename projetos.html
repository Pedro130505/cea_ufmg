Aircraft & Projects

Explore the aircraft, prototypes, student design studies and collaborative projects documented in CEA’s archive. Each entry connects its engineering purpose with the people, construction work and learning process behind it.

From Cláudio Barros’s early gliders to later experimental aircraft, CEA’s projects connect generations of students and teachers. Designing an aircraft means relating requirements and calculations to materials, manufacturing, instrumentation and testing.

CEA-101 • 1964

CB.1 Gaivota

Single-Seat Basic Training Glider — PP-ZTZ

First prototype engineered at CEA by Prof. Cláudio Barros and completed in 1964. Built from Freijó wood and aeronautical plywood covered in cotton fabric, it served as the foundational milestone of UFMG's practical laboratory.

CEA-102 • 1975

CB.2 Minuano

High-Performance Single-Seat Sailplane — PP-ZPZ

High-performance glider built between 1972 and 1974, first flown in December 1975. Pioneer in bonded metal-to-metal and wood-to-metal structural joints with cellulose acetate honeycomb sandwich, achieving a 38:1 glide ratio.

CB.7 Vesper

Two-Seat Tandem Motor-Glider — PT-ZCB

Collaborative programme between CEA and CETEC/SP initiated in 1985 and flown in 1988. Two-seat twin-boom composite motor-glider expanding CEA's structural experience with composite materials.

CB-10 • Triathlon

CB-10 Triathlon

Two-Seat Side-by-Side Aerobatic Aircraft

Two-seat side-by-side low-wing airplane with retractable tricycle gear. Resulted from Cláudio Barros's doctoral methodology for light-aircraft design. Completed and flown in 2005 by José Celso Calegari from CEA plans.

Methodology

From a Design Decision to Evidence

At introductory level, students define missions, review inherited designs and support their choices with calculations. As they progress, aircraft development adds detailed engineering, construction, instrumentation and experimental evaluation. Conceptual coursework and completed aircraft are presented as different kinds of achievement.
