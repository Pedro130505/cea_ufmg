High-performance experimental aircraft designed and built by engineering students for physical flight validation and world record challenges.

CEA-101 • 1964

CB.1 Gaivota

Single-Seat Basic Training Glider — PP-ZTZ

First prototype engineered at CEA by Prof. Cláudio Barros and completed in 1964. Built from Freijó wood and aeronautical plywood covered in cotton fabric, it served as the foundational milestone of UFMG's practical laboratory.

CEA-102 • 1974

CB.2 Minuano

High-Performance Single-Seat Sailplane — PP-ZPZ

Direct evolution of the Gaivota, the Minuano utilized highly refined laminar flow airfoils and a composite wood-fiberglass structure, achieving a 38:1 glide ratio. Won the 1978 Brazilian National Soaring Championship.

CB.7 Vesper

Two-Seat Tandem Motor-Glider — PT-ZCB

Joint project between CEA-UFMG and CETEC/SP. Tandem twin-boom configuration combining high-efficiency sailplane aerodynamics with self-launch capability for long-duration atmospheric research.

Pinnacle of CEA engineering: digitally designed and built with advanced composite tooling. Holds five FAI world records, including 521.08 km/h — the fastest speed ever registered by a piston aircraft under 500kg.

CEA-205 • CB.9 Curumim

CB.9 Curumim

Single-Seat Training Aircraft — ROTAX 532 Engine

Built at CEA from Freijó wood and pine plywood, Curumim was the main powered flight trainer for the lab. Featuring low stall speed (50 km/h) and short takeoff capability, it was extensively used for testing student avionics and telemetry.
