Founder of CEA · 1937–2011

Cláudio Barros: A School of Aircraft Engineering

Cláudio Pinto de Barros established a way of teaching engineering through the design, construction and testing of real aircraft. His work at CEA connected technical reasoning with workshop decisions, teamwork and responsibility. The aircraft, the engineers he helped educate and the continuity of that practical approach form the centre of this tribute.

Chronological Heritage

Life, Method & Aircraft Development

1937–1964 · FOUNDATION & FIRST FLIGHT

Origins and Gaivota

Cláudio Pinto de Barros was born in Curvelo, Minas Gerais, in 1937. While studying engineering in Belo Horizonte, he pursued aircraft design using Stelio Frati’s L’Aliante as a reference for the Gaivota glider. In 1963, he founded CEA. The wooden Gaivota first flew in 1964, bringing together engineering analysis, aircraft construction and flight experience.

1969–1975 · EXPANDING STRUCTURAL HORIZONS

Developing a Practical School

Work on the Minuano began in 1969. Built by José de Carvalho e Silva, the glider extended CEA’s experience with bonded structures and lightweight sandwich construction. It first flew in December 1975. Aircraft projects gave students a way to connect calculations, construction decisions and the behaviour of a working machine.

1980s–1993 · COMPOSITES & POWERED AIRCRAFT

New Materials and New Aircraft

During the 1980s, the Vesper programme expanded CEA’s experience with composite structures. Development began in 1985 and the aircraft first flew in 1988. The two-seat Curumim followed, with development between 1989 and 1992 and its first flight in 1993. Each project added technical experience to the educational foundation established through the earlier gliders.

1997 · METHODOLOGY & FORMALIZATION

Making Design Knowledge Transferable

After a period at Universidade da Beira Interior in Portugal, Cláudio returned to UFMG in 1997. His doctoral work, supervised by former student Ricardo Utsch, developed a methodology for light-aircraft design. The associated book proposal brought together design reasoning, calculations and construction solutions for the CB-10 Triathlon.

2004–2011 · LASTING LEGACY

A Lasting Educational Legacy

Cláudio retired in 2004 and continued contributing to CEA as a volunteer. In 2005, José Celso Calegari completed and flew a privately built Triathlon from the CEA design. Cláudio died on 2 July 2011. His work left an educational culture in which developing aircraft and educating engineers remained closely connected.

Foundational Method · The Akaflieg Tradition

Learning by Designing, Building and Flying

CEA’s approach draws on the educational tradition of Germany’s Akafliegs, academic flying groups that connect aircraft development with construction and flying. The value lies in linking activities often taught separately in conventional engineering curricula.

01

Analytical Conception & Calculation

Translating mission specifications and aerodynamic physics into precise mathematical formulations and structural sizing.

02

Workshop Hardware Fabrication

Students turn drawings into physical parts—cutting ribs, bonding spars, curing composites, and solving hands-on manufacturing challenges.

03

Flight Validation & Feedback

Confronting theoretical predictions with direct piloting experience, telemetry measurements, and real atmospheric behaviour.

Living Heritage

The School Continues

// FACULTY CONTINUITY

Academic Succession

The school Cláudio helped establish continued through the work of professors including Rogério Pinto Ribeiro, Eduardo Bauzer Medeiros, Ricardo Utsch and Paulo Iscold. Their contributions connected aircraft design with construction, analysis, instrumentation and teaching. Iscold’s work at CEA belongs to this historical context. He is now at California Polytechnic State University, San Luis Obispo.

// MEMORY & COMMUNITY

Family, Memory & AeroCB

Cláudio’s family and recurring encounters between generations help sustain CEA’s memory. Maria Cecília Pereira has played an important and recurring role in organising AeroCB from its beginnings. Her contribution is part of the collective work that brings students, teachers, professionals, collaborators and the family together.

// CURRENT HORIZON

Continuing the Approach

Today, CEA’s coordination brings complementary emphases to this tradition. Joel continues aircraft construction and development, while Luiz supports its educational continuity through student projects and connections with industry.
