Tribute to Prof. Cláudio Barros

The visionary mentor who taught UFMG to design wings and challenge world flight records.

The Founder

Prof. Cláudio Pinto de Barros

Professor Cláudio Pinto de Barros was an absolute visionary in Brazilian aeronautical engineering. In 1963, recognizing that purely theoretical classroom lectures constrained the full potential of future aircraft designers, he established the Center for Aeronautical Studies (CEA) at UFMG.

Inspired by traditional German Akafliegs (student academic groups that voluntarily designed and built advanced sailplanes), Barros introduced this model to Brazil. Under his guidance, students engaged in hands-on engineering: from manual drafting boards to shaping Freijó wood and molding complex composite airframes.

Throughout his career, he spearheaded the construction of aircraft prototypes bearing the "CB" prefix (his initials). His legacy of technical rigor and experimental audacity paved the way for subsequent generations of world record-breaking aircraft designed by Paulo Iscold (his advisee) and built by UFMG students.

"An aeronautical engineer is not formed solely by calculations on drafting boards. He is truly forged upon watching prototype wood and metal take physical shape and finally soar into the skies."

— Prof. Cláudio Barros
Memories & Testimonials

Words from Those Who Knew Him

Students, advisees, and colleagues share their memories of Prof. Cláudio — originally published on the CEA-UFMG blog.

Prof. Cláudio was not just an aeronautical engineering professor; he was a life exemplar, an inspiration, a friend. Beyond his 12 aircraft projects, beyond biennials and world records, his true legacy was the numerous engineers he helped form and prepare. We will fly higher, farther, and faster!

— Paulo Iscold, mentee and CEA successor

More than an aircraft design professor, Cláudio was a mentor of life. Whenever I faced difficult decisions during my years at CEA, he was the person I turned to. His guidance shaped our career choices and personal integrity.

— Antonio Rafael, CEA alumnus

I had the privilege of being part of one of his last graduating classes in Aeronautical Engineering. When I discussed my academic struggles with him, he handed me his annotated Bible and wrote a dedication encouraging perseverance. I carry his wisdom to this day.

— José de Ávila Jr., CEA alumnus & Ph.D.

Uncle Cláudio built the esprit de corps that unites all UFMG Aeronautical Engineers into a close family. We all celebrate each other's achievements and proudly connect our successes to our school.

— Pável Bersan, CEA alumnus

With a dream to build an airplane and an Italian book on glider design, Cláudio got to work in 1964 and completed his first glider: the CB-1 Gaivota. Visionaries like him write their own history.

— Gustavo Junqueira, CEA alumnus

His achievements ensure that his legacy will outlast decades and generations. As Guimarães Rosa wrote, great teachers do not die; they remain enchanted in the minds of those they inspired.

— Renato Ruas, CEA alumnus
