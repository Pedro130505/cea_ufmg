CEA-101 · CB.1 • 1964

CEA-101 CB.1 Gaivota

Single-Seat Basic Training Glider — PP-ZTZ

First aircraft engineered at CEA by Prof. Cláudio Barros and completed in 1964. Built from Brazilian freijó wood and aeronautical plywood covered in cotton fabric, it served as the foundational cornerstone of UFMG's practical aeronautical workshop.

Fully restored to flight status between 2001 and 2003, the Gaivota represents the origin of experimental flight testing at UFMG. Designed specifically for ab-initio glider training, it features a 12-meter high-wing wood structure engineered for structural simplicity and ease of field repair.

CEA-102 · CB.2 • 1975

CEA-102 CB.2 Minuano

High-Performance Standard Class Sailplane — PP-ZPZ

Designed in 1969 and built between 1972 and 1974, Minuano made its maiden flight in December 1975 at Lagoa Santa. A pioneer in structural engineering, it incorporated bonded metal-to-metal and wood-to-metal joints with cellulose acetate honeycomb sandwich skins.

Minuano achieved an exceptional 38:1 glide ratio, establishing new standards for Brazilian gliding. The project validated advanced structural bonding techniques and laminar-flow aerodynamic airfoils under demanding flight envelope conditions.

CETEC-303 · CB.7 • 1988

CETEC-303 CB.7 Vesper

Two-Seat Tandem Composite Motor-Glider — PT-ZCB

Collaborative programme between CEA and CETEC/SP initiated in 1985 and flown in 1988. Featuring an innovative twin-boom pusher configuration manufactured in composite materials.

The Vesper received the prestigious international design award at the 1990 OSTIV Congress in France. Its composite structure and pusher-propeller arrangement minimized cabin noise while maximizing aerodynamic efficiency.

CEA-104 · RPR.1 • 1980

CEA-104 RPR.1 Jegue

Single-Seat Self-Launching Sailplane

Conceived by student Rogério Pinto Ribeiro in 1979–1980 within the Aircraft Design academic curriculum as a lightweight single-seat self-launching glider featuring an auxiliary powerplant.

Designed to enable autonomous soaring operations without a towplane, the Jegue integrated a retractable auxiliary engine system, pioneering self-launching sailplane concepts in Brazilian academia.

CEA-205 · CB.9 • 1993

CEA-205 CB.9 Curumim

Two-Seat Touring Monoplane & Flying Laboratory

Developed between 1989 and 1992, winning the 1992 Brazilian Ultralight Association Design Prize before making its maiden flight in 1993. Built from freijó wood and pine plywood.

Curumim served as an ab initio flight trainer and an instrumented flying laboratory for student research, telemetry calibration, and flight parameter identification over hundreds of flight hours.

CEA-308 • 2002 · 4× FAI World Records

CEA-308

Single-Seat World Record High-Efficiency Monoplane

Conceived in early 1999 and built between January 2000 and 2001, CEA-308 completed its maiden flight on 14 May 2002 at Confins, piloted by Daniel Aninger de Barros. Powered by an 80-hp Jabiru 2200 engine.

It established four official FAI world records — three speed records and one time-to-climb record — in Class C-1.a/0 under pilot Gunar Armin Halboth, proving world-class aerodynamic efficiency.

CEA-309 • 2003 / 2009

CEA-309 Mehari

Single-Seat Advanced Unlimited Aerobatic Monoplane

Construction commenced in March 2003 at the CEA workshop, with inaugural flight evaluations completed at Pampulha Airport in 2009. Designed for unlimited competition aerobatics.

Engineered for high structural load limits (+10/-10g), featuring a symmetrical wing section, carbon composite control surfaces, and extreme roll rate capability.

CEA-311 • 2015 · 5× FAI World Records

CEA-311 Anequim

All-Carbon Racing Monoplane · 521 km/h Record

Pure carbon-fiber racing monoplane engineered at CEA. In August 2015, piloted by Gunar Armin Halboth at the Santa Cruz Air Force Base in Rio de Janeiro, Anequim established five official FAI world speed and climb records.

Reaching 521 km/h in Class C-1.b (sub-500 kg), Anequim became the world's fastest four-cylinder piston aircraft, showcasing peak computational CFD aerodynamics and carbon sandwich optimization.
